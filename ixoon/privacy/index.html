Lune Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 November 2026

Lune is a music player for iPhone Duo and iPhone. It plays your Apple Music subscription and library, and your own Navidrome or Subsonic server if you add one, one at a time. It is a player over music you already have, not a music service of its own. This page says plainly what the app does with your information.

The short version

  • Lune collects nothing about you. There are no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and the developer receives no data from the app.
  • The app talks to two places only: Apple, when you use Apple Music, and your own server, at the address you entered, if you add one. Nothing goes anywhere else.
  • The app only ever sends what playback needs and what you ask it to do, such as starring a song. Scrobbling is off unless you switch it on.
  • There are no accounts of the app's own. You sign in to your music, not to Lune.

Apple Music

When you play Apple Music, the app uses Apple's MusicKit. Your requests, such as a search of your library or of Apple Music's catalog, a browse of your library, the notes about an album or artist whose page you open, or a track to play, go from your iPhone to Apple, and Apple handles them under Apple's Privacy Policy. Artwork comes from Apple too. iOS asks your permission before the app can see your music library. When you ask the app to save the queue as a playlist, add a song to a playlist or add a song or an album to your library, it asks Apple to do it, and only then. The developer never sees your Apple Account, your library or what you play. Lune never sells or gates access to Apple Music; you pay Apple for your subscription and the app plays it.

Your own server

You can add a Navidrome or other Subsonic server of your own. The address and username you enter are kept in the app's settings on your iPhone, and the password (or the server's API key, where it offers one) is kept in the iPhone Keychain. They are sent only to the address you entered, over HTTPS, and nowhere else. Each request carries your username and either a token made from your password, rather than the password itself, or the API key.

The app asks your server for:

  • your library: artists, albums, songs, playlists, your starred items, your searches, and the server's own lists such as recently added and most played;
  • cover art, which comes only from your server;
  • the notes about an album and the biography of an artist whose page you open, as text;
  • songs like an artist, or like the last song in your queue, when you start a station or switch on play on;
  • the music itself: each track you play is streamed from your server, as the original file or, on a mobile network, at a quality you choose in the app;
  • downloads: albums, playlists and songs you choose to download, or keep on your iPhone with Keep Starred Music or Keep on This iPhone, are copied from your server and kept on your iPhone. They use a mobile network only if you switch on Download over cellular.

The app changes something on your server only when you tap to: starring or unstarring a song, album or artist, saving the queue as a playlist, or adding a song to a playlist.

Scrobbling is off unless you switch it on, on the sign-in form or later in the server's settings in the app. When it is on, the app tells your server what you have started playing and, once you have played half a track (or four minutes of it), that you played it and when. Plays made while you are offline wait on your iPhone and are sent to your server later. The app sends plays to your server only. Your server may pass them on to ListenBrainz, Last.fm or another service, but only if you linked one on the server. That is outside the app and under the terms of the service you chose.

Your server is yours, or run by someone you chose. Whoever runs it can see these requests, as with any server: what was streamed, downloaded or searched for, the stars and playlist changes, the plays if scrobbling is on, the network address the requests came from, and that they came from Lune (which your server lists as iXoon, the name it has known the app by). That is between you and your server. The developer has no access to your server and never sees any of it.

The app never contacts Last.fm, ListenBrainz, MusicBrainz or any other service itself. Your server may, under its own settings: Navidrome, for example, can fetch artist pictures, information and similar songs from outside services, and pass plays on to services you linked. That traffic is your server's, not the app's.

Kept on your iPhone

Downloads, cover art the app has already shown, the queue, your pins and preferences, the server's settings and plays waiting to be sent to your server are kept in the app's own storage on your iPhone. Downloads are left out of iCloud Backup. None of it is sent to the developer.

The app also keeps a listening log on your iPhone. Each time a song from Apple Music or your server has played for half its length (or four minutes), it notes the song, artist and album, the source's ids for them, and when you started it. The log is on unless you switch it off in Customize, where you can also delete it. It is part of your iPhone's backup (iCloud Backup or a computer, if you use one), like the rest of the app's storage, and the app never sends it anywhere.

Widget, Lock Screen, Siri and Shortcuts

The Home Screen and StandBy widget shows what is playing from a small file the app shares with it on your iPhone. Its cover comes from the music library on your iPhone or from cover art the app already has. The Lock Screen and Control Center show what is playing through iOS. Siri and the Shortcuts actions, such as asking what is playing or playing an album by name, run in the app on your iPhone and act only when you ask; what you say to Siri is handled by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy. None of this sends anything to the developer.

iCloud

If the app syncs its own settings between your devices, it uses your private iCloud database, which only your Apple Account can read. The developer cannot see it. Apple runs iCloud under Apple's Privacy Policy. Your music and your server credentials are never put in iCloud by the app.

What never happens

  • No analytics or tracking software of any kind, from the developer or anyone else.
  • No advertising.
  • No accounts beyond your music sources and your own iCloud.
  • No artwork or artist pictures fetched from the open web. Artwork comes from Apple Music or from your own server.
  • No contact with Last.fm, ListenBrainz or any other service by the app itself.
  • Nothing is sold or shared. The developer collects nothing, so there is nothing to sell or share.

Crash reports reach the developer only if you have chosen, in iOS Settings under Privacy & Security, to share them with app developers. That is Apple's setting, and the reports Apple passes on carry no personal information.

Purchases

The app is free. If you buy the Full Pass, Apple handles the purchase, the payment and the receipt through the App Store. The app only keeps a note on your iPhone that the pass is yours, and sends nothing about it anywhere. The developer never sees your payment details and keeps nothing about your purchase.

Children

Lune collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

Any change to what leaves your iPhone is published here, with a new date, in the same release as the change. The app will never start collecting your information without saying so first.
